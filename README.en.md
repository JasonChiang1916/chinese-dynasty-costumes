## About

This repository contains the complete **Chinese Dynasty Costume Atlas**: 760 costume plates covering 19 dynasties, from the Shang dynasty to the Republic of China. Each dynasty includes 10 outfits for adult men, 10 for adult women, 10 for boys and 10 for girls — 40 outfits per dynasty — presenting the evolution of Chinese dress from the Bronze Age to the modern era.

All images are AI-generated photorealistic costume plates following one consistent display standard: the "headless ghost mannequin" presentation — light grey background, full-body front view, garments holding their worn shape on their own, with no head, no face, no hands, no mannequin body, no stand, no hanger and no text in the image.

## The 40 slots per dynasty

Each dynasty follows the same 40-slot framework, with styles adapted to the period:

**Adult men (M01–M10):** M01 monarch's ceremonial robe, M02 civil official's court robe, M03 general's armor, M04 scholar's everyday robe, M05 young nobleman's formal dress, M06 wealthy merchant, M07 commoner's everyday dress, M08 farmer/worker, M09 guard, soldier or servant, M10 wedding robe

**Adult women (F01–F10):** F01 empress's ceremonial robe, F02 noblewoman's formal dress, F03 palace maid, F04 young noblewoman's formal dress, F05 gentlewoman's everyday dress, F06 merchant's wife, F07 common woman's everyday dress, F08 working woman, F09 singer/dancer, F10 bridal dress

**Boys (B01–B10):** prince, young nobleman, official's son, schoolboy, common boy, farm boy, festival dress, ritual dress, young martial arts student, servant boy

**Girls (G01–G10):** princess, young noblewoman, official's daughter, young lady of the household, common girl, farm girl, festival dress, ritual dress, young performer, maid

Children's outfits are ordinary historical children's clothing, modest and properly fitted.

## File naming

Every file name carries the dynasty, gender, adult/child, slot number and style name:

```
{Dynasty}_{Adult/Child}{Male/Female}_{Slot}_{Style}.webp
```

Example: `唐_成年男_M01_君主大礼服.webp` (Tang, adult male, M01, monarch's ceremonial robe).

Revised versions carry a version suffix after the style name (e.g. `_v2`) and never overwrite earlier versions.

## Display standard

All 760 plates follow the same standard:

- Headless ghost-mannequin presentation, light grey background, full-body front view, photorealistic
- No head, no face, no hands, no mannequin body, no stand, no hanger
- Garments hold their own worn shape; top and trousers/skirt join at the waist with no gap
- Where the hem meets the shoe, legs are not exposed; shoes sit under the hem, or a real ankle/foot connects into the shoe
- No flat lay, no plastic dummy, no separated pieces, no text in the image

Additional notes:

- Tang dynasty everyday, noble and dancer women's wear follows the high-Tang open-collar, high-waisted style; formal ceremonial robes remain modest
- In pre-modern Chinese dress the calf is not exposed: hems reach the shoe, or white socks / tall boots cover the ankle
- Noblemen's footwear: decorated slippers (舄) before the Tang, boots from the Tang onward; black lace-up leather shoes for the Republic of China
